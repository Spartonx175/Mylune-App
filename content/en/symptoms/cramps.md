  - The cramps come at regular intervals, or your tummy keeps tightening.
  - You have lower back pain.
  - You notice unusual discharge or fluid leaking.
  - It hurts when you pass urine, or your urine is cloudy, pink, red or brown.
  - The pain is severe, or it does not ease after 30 to 60 minutes of rest.
  - You have strong pain in your shoulder tip, or you feel faint. Call 15 or 112.
  - Moving is hard, or turning over in bed or climbing stairs hurts (pelvic pain).

Mild tummy pain is common in pregnancy. It often comes from stretching ligaments, constipation or trapped wind.

Mild pain that eases when you change position, rest, pass wind or open your bowels is probably harmless.

## What can help

- Change position, or lie down for a while.
- Rest, and see if the pain goes away.
- Look at the constipation page if your bowels are slow.

If you are worried about any pain, call your maternity unit, midwife or doctor. You do not need to wait for your next appointment.
