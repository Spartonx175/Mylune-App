This page helps you decide when to call. It does not tell you what is wrong. If you are worried about anything, call your maternity unit, midwife or doctor. You do not need to wait for your next appointment.

## Emergency numbers (France)

- **15 (SAMU)** or **112**: emergency services. Use them if there is an immediate danger.
- **3114**: national line for mental health distress and suicide prevention. Free, day and night, every day. Call it if you are in psychological distress.

Sources: ameli-crise-suicidaire, ameli-baby-blues

## Call 15 or 112 now

- You are bleeding heavily and feel faint or unwell.
- You have bleeding with strong pain in your belly or pelvis, pain in your shoulder tip, or you feel like fainting.
- You have thoughts of suicide that will not go away, a plan, or a way to hurt yourself within reach.

Sources: ameli-bleeding-t1, nhs-vaginal-bleeding, ameli-crise-suicidaire

## Bleeding

- In the first 3 months: heavy bleeding, bleeding with pain in the lower belly or pelvis (one side or both), bleeding with fever, or passing brownish tissue. Go to urgent care now.
- After the first 3 months: any bleeding is not normal. Go straight to your maternity unit, or call the emergency services.
- Light spotting without pain: call your maternity unit, midwife or doctor and ask for an appointment soon.

Sources: ameli-bleeding-t1, ameli-bleeding-t2t3, nhs-vaginal-bleeding

## Pain low in the belly early in pregnancy

- Pain low in the belly, often on one side, with or without light dark bleeding, early in pregnancy. Get seen urgently. Sometimes there is no pain at all.

Sources: ameli-ectopic, nhs-vaginal-bleeding

## Signs of high blood pressure in pregnancy (usually after 20 SA)

Call your maternity unit or go there now if you have any of these:
- a headache that does not go away
- blurred or double vision, flashing lights or spots
- pain in a band across the upper belly or under the ribs
- sudden swelling of the face, hands or feet
- feeling sick or being sick, or passing much less urine
- feeling very unwell

Sources: ameli-preeclampsia, nhs-preeclampsia, nhs-symptoms-get-help

## Your baby moves less

- If your baby moves less than usual, you cannot feel movements, or the usual pattern changes: call your maternity unit or midwife straight away, even at night. Do not wait until the next day. Do not use a home listening device to check.
- Most women feel movements between 16 and 24 weeks. If you have felt none by 24 weeks, tell your midwife.

Sources: nhs-baby-movements, nhs-symptoms-get-help

## Itching

- Very itchy skin that keeps you awake, or itching of the palms of the hands or soles of the feet, especially at night: call your maternity unit.

Sources: nhs-itching, nhs-symptoms-get-help

## Being sick all the time

- You cannot keep food or drink down, you feel dizzy, thirsty, or pass little dark urine: contact your midwife, doctor or hospital as soon as possible.

Sources: nhs-severe-vomiting, nhs-symptoms-get-help

## Low mood, anxiety or thoughts of harming yourself

- Constant or very strong anxiety, panic, deep sadness or despair, or feeling you cannot look after yourself: talk to your doctor or midwife soon. You are not alone and it can be treated.
- Free, day and night: **3114**.
- If the thoughts are strong and you could act on them: **15** or **112**.

Sources: ameli-mental-health, ameli-crise-suicidaire, ameli-baby-blues
