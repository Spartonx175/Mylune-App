baby:
  - Your baby is about 41 mm long, roughly the size of a fig.
  - Fingers and toes are separating, and tiny nails are forming.
  - The ears are small but in place.
  - Your baby kicks, but you will not feel it for several weeks.
  - The placenta is about to take over from the yolk sac.
mother:
  - You may feel hot, sweaty or dizzy, because you have more blood.
  - Stretching muscles and ligaments can cause some tummy pain.
  - You may still feel tired and emotional.
todo:
  - Your first ultrasound can be done from this week. It takes place between 11 SA and 13 SA plus 6 days.
  - Ask what the ultrasound will check. It dates the pregnancy, shows if there is one baby or more, and measures the neck fold (nuchal translucency).
  - Ask about the blood test that can be combined with the ultrasound for trisomy 21 screening.
  - Make sure your declaration of pregnancy is sent before the end of the 3rd month.

  - Go for a gentle walk. It can help with tiredness.
  - Stand up slowly if you feel dizzy.
  - Dress in layers, so you can cool down.
  - Drink water through the day.
  - Eat regular meals.
  - Tell your partner what you feel. They want to help.
  - Prepare questions for the ultrasound.

At 11 SA (SG 9), your baby is about the size of a fig. Fingers and toes are separating, and nails are starting to grow.

Your body has more blood now, so you may feel warm or a bit dizzy. Mild tummy pain can come from stretching muscles. If the pain is severe, tell your doctor or midwife quickly.

This week, the first ultrasound can take place. It may be the first time you see your baby.
